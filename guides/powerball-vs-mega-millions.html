Powerball vs Mega Millions: Odds, Jackpots & Which One to Play

Lottery Guide · NumberBuzz USA

Powerball and Mega Millions sit side by side in every gas station in America, and both regularly offer jackpots in the hundreds of millions — so what is actually different? The short answer: Powerball draws three nights a week (Monday, Wednesday, Saturday) and costs $2 a play, while Mega Millions draws two nights a week (Tuesday, Friday) and costs $5 a play, with slightly different number pools and odds. Together they cover six nights a week, so there is almost always a big draw tonight or tomorrow.

Draw days and times

Powerball drawings are held Monday, Wednesday and Saturday at 10:59 p.m. Eastern Time. Mega Millions drawings are held Tuesday and Friday at 11:00 p.m. Eastern. That is why NumberBuzz lists both games side by side on the homepage: check in the morning and you will see last night’s result plus the next jackpot for whichever game plays next. Our guides to the Powerball drawing time and the Mega Millions drawing schedule cover the exact times in every US time zone.

The number pools

Powerball asks you to pick 5 numbers from 1–69 plus a red Powerball from 1–26. Mega Millions asks for 5 numbers from 1–70 plus a gold Mega Ball from 1–24. The pools look similar, but the slightly larger Mega Millions white-ball pool and its smaller bonus-ball pool combine to make its jackpot odds a touch longer.

The odds

The jackpot odds are 1 in 292.2 million for Powerball and 1 in 290.5 million for Mega Millions — close enough that neither game is meaningfully “easier” at the very top. Where the games feel different is the smaller prizes. Both pay for matching only the bonus ball ($4 in Powerball; in Mega Millions the Mega Ball alone now pays more, thanks to the built-in multiplier), both pay $1 million for matching all five main numbers in Powerball — Mega Millions’ Match 5 pays $2 million to $10 million with its multiplier — and both have a ladder of prizes in between. Your overall chance of winning any prize is roughly 1 in 24 in Powerball and 1 in 23 in Mega Millions.

Jackpots, rollovers and starting points

Both jackpots grow — “roll over” — whenever nobody matches all the numbers, and both reset after a win. Powerball restarts at $20 million; Mega Millions restarts at $50 million, one reason its headline number often looks bigger. The reset is also why the two jackpots on this page can look wildly different on the same morning: one game may have just been won while the other has been rolling for weeks. Both games pay the jackpot as a 30-year annuity or as a smaller lump-sum cash option.

The multipliers

Powerball’s Power Play costs an extra $1 and multiplies most non-jackpot prizes by 2×–10×. Mega Millions took a different route in its current format: a multiplier is built into every $5 ticket at no extra cost, boosting non-jackpot prizes by 2×–10× automatically. Either way, multipliers never change the jackpot itself — they only fatten the smaller tiers, which is where nearly all winners actually land.

Which should you play?

Honestly: whichever jackpot is bigger, whichever draw is tonight — or simply the game whose numbers you like. Statistically the two games are near-identical, and every combination in both games has exactly the same chance as every other combination. If you play both, our ticket-checking guide shows the careful way to check either ticket. Play for fun, spend only what you can afford to lose, and always verify results with the official lottery.

What a long rollover streak does to both games

When either jackpot climbs past half a billion dollars, ticket sales surge — and the jackpot grows faster with every roll, because the prize is funded by sales. That is also when office pools and casual players pile in, which raises the chance that a winning jackpot is shared between several tickets. The odds of your numbers being drawn never change; what changes is how many other people are holding numbers alongside you.

Frequently asked questions

Which game has better jackpot odds?

Mega Millions, by a hair: 1 in 290.5 million versus Powerball’s 1 in 292.2 million. In practice the difference is meaningless — both are roughly the same as flipping a coin and getting the same result 28 times in a row.

Can I play both games on the same night?

They never draw on the same night — Powerball plays Monday, Wednesday and Saturday; Mega Millions plays Tuesday and Friday — so across a week you can enter all five drawings.

Which jackpot grows faster?

It depends on ticket sales. Bigger jackpots sell more tickets, which makes any jackpot snowball faster the higher it climbs. Mega Millions starts higher after a reset ($50 million versus $20 million).
