Wordle Tips: Best Starting Words & How to Keep Your Streak

Daily Fun Guide · NumberBuzz USA

The fastest way to get better at Wordle is to start smarter: open with a word packed with common vowels and consonants — CRANE, SLATE, CRATE, TRACE or AUDIO are the classic choices — then let the colored tiles eliminate letters systematically instead of guessing favorite words. Wordle gives you six tries to find a five-letter word; most losses come not from hard words but from wasted guesses. Here is how strong players structure all six. (Just want today’s answer? How the colors actually work

Green means the letter is in the word and in the right place. Yellow means the letter is in the word but in a different place. Grey means the letter is not in the word at all — with one trap: if a word contains a letter twice and you guessed it twice, one instance can show grey even though the letter is in the word. For example, if the answer has one E and your guess had two, you may get one green or yellow E and one grey E. Read duplicates carefully before you discard a letter.

Pick a starting word like a scientist

A great opener tests five different, very common letters. The most frequent letters in English five-letter words are E, A, R, O, T, L, I and S. That is why CRANE (C-R-A-N-E), SLATE (S-L-A-T-E) and CRATE score so well in computer analyses, and why AUDIO — four vowels at once — is a favorite of players who like to map the vowels first. There is no single mathematically perfect opener for human players; the best starting word is a strong one you use every day, so its results become a familiar decision tree.

The second guess matters most

Your second guess should use five brand-new letters unless the first guess hit green. If SLATE gave you nothing, a follow-up like COURI or PRION sweeps up the remaining vowels and common consonants. Resist the urge to immediately chase a yellow letter with a word full of repeats — information wins early games; solving can wait until guess three or four.

Mid-game: think in patterns, not words

By guess three, stop brainstorming random words and start listing what the answer could be: fixed greens, yellow letters that must move, and dead letters excluded. Watch for common endings (-ER, -ED, -LY, -EST patterns like -IGHT) and common structures — double letters appear far more often than players expect (LL, SS, EE, OO). If you are stuck between several possible answers that differ by one letter (LIGHT, MIGHT, RIGHT, TIGHT), spend a guess on a word that tests several of those differing letters at once instead of betting on one.

Hard mode and streaks

Hard mode forces you to reuse revealed hints, which feels pure but actually raises your loss rate in those one-letter-apart traps, because you cannot play a letter-testing word. If your streak matters to you, normal mode is the safer home. And about streaks: they live in your browser or app account — clearing cookies or switching devices can reset the counter even if you never lose, which has broken more hearts than any difficult word.

A simple three-guess framework

Put it together and a strong game looks like this. Guess 1: your fixed opener (say, CRATE). Guess 2: five new letters that mop up the remaining vowels and common consonants — unless guess 1 landed two or more greens, in which case build around them. Guess 3: the first genuine solving attempt, using everything learned. From guess 4 onward, list every candidate the tiles still allow before typing anything; if several candidates differ by one letter, spend one guess testing the differing letters instead of gambling. That discipline — information first, glory second — is the entire difference between a 98% win rate and a broken streak.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best Wordle starting word?

For humans, CRANE, SLATE and CRATE are the most recommended — five common letters, two vowels, strong follow-up paths. AUDIO is the best vowel-hunting opener. Consistency matters more than the tiny differences between them.

Can a Wordle answer repeat a letter?

Yes — answers can and do contain double letters (think SASSY or EERIE-style patterns), and letters can also repeat across different positions. Never assume all five letters are unique.

Do answers ever repeat?

The New York Times curates the answer list and has avoided repeats so far; the list is also edited to remove obscure and offensive words, so very rare words you might guess will never be the answer (though they may be accepted as guesses).
