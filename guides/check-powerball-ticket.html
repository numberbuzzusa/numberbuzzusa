How to Check Your Powerball Ticket (Step by Step)

Lottery Guide · NumberBuzz USA

Checking a Powerball ticket sounds simple, yet every year prizes go unclaimed because someone misread a number, forgot that the Powerball alone can win a prize, or threw a ticket away too early. The careful way takes less than a minute: confirm the draw date, match the five white balls in any order, check the red Powerball separately, and then verify your result a second time against an official source before you celebrate — or before you bin the ticket.

Step 1: Check the draw date first

Powerball drawings are held every Monday, Wednesday and Saturday at 10:59 p.m. Eastern Time. Your ticket is only valid for the draw date printed on it, unless you bought a multi-draw ticket covering several dates. Compare your numbers against the result for that exact date — on the NumberBuzz homepage, the draw date is always printed right next to the game name, and last night’s numbers are posted every morning.

Step 2: Match the five white balls — order does not matter

Your five main numbers (from 1–69) can match the five white balls in any order. Count your matches slowly, one number at a time, and tick each one off on the ticket itself. Then check the red Powerball (from 1–26) separately. Many players are surprised by how many ways there are to win: matching the red Powerball alone already pays $4, and there are nine prize tiers in total.

If you added the $1 Power Play option, most non-jackpot prizes are multiplied by 2, 3, 4, 5 or even 10 times, depending on the multiplier drawn that night. The Match 5 prize is the exception: with Power Play it is always doubled to $2 million, whatever the multiplier.

Step 3: Double-check before you celebrate — or bin it

Use the free ticket checker on our homepage to compare your numbers instantly — nothing you type is saved or sent anywhere. Then verify the result a second time against the official Powerball or your state lottery listing. Results on NumberBuzz are unofficial, and the official source is the only one that counts for a claim. Keep the physical ticket until you have done both checks, and remember that unclaimed prizes expire — typically 90 days to one year after the draw, depending on the state where you bought the ticket.

If you win: the first 24 hours

Sign the back of the ticket immediately — an unsigned winning ticket can, in most places, be claimed by whoever holds it. Photograph both sides and store the original somewhere safe, like a lockbox. For small prizes (usually up to $600), your retailer can normally pay you on the spot. For larger prizes, slow down: stay quiet, take your time, and speak to a lawyer and a financial adviser before claiming. Most states give you at least 180 days, so there is no need to rush. You will also have to decide between the lump-sum cash option and the annuity paid over 30 years — our guide to the first 7 things to do if you win walks through that decision, and the jackpot tax calculator on the homepage gives a rough first estimate of what each choice means after tax.

Multi-draw and advance-play tickets

If you bought a multi-draw ticket, it stays alive across several drawings — check it after every draw it covers, not just the last one, and keep it safe in the meantime (a photo of both sides is a good backup, though only the original ticket can be claimed). Scratch the habit of checking once and binning: a ticket that wins nothing on Monday can still win on Wednesday. When the final covered draw has passed and both checks are done, the ticket has done its job.

Frequently asked questions

Can I check an old Powerball ticket?

Yes — as long as it is still within your state’s claim deadline (90 days to one year after the draw). Check the numbers against the result for the exact draw date printed on the ticket, not the latest draw.

What if I only matched the Powerball?

You still win $4, which covers the cost of the ticket plus a dollar — or two more plays. It is the most commonly missed prize, so always check the red ball.

Does the order of the numbers matter?

No. Your five white-ball numbers can match in any order. Only the count of matches — and whether the Powerball matches — decides your prize.
