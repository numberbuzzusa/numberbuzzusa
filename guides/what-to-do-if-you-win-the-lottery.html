Won the Lottery? The First 7 Things to Do

Lottery Guide · NumberBuzz USA

You checked your ticket twice, your hands are shaking, and the numbers match: you have won the lottery. What you do in the next days matters almost as much as the win itself. The calm, correct order is: sign the ticket, keep it safe, tell almost nobody, hire a lawyer and a tax professional, understand your claim deadline, decide lump sum versus annuity with real advice, and only then claim. Here is each step in plain English.

1. Sign the ticket and secure it

Before anything else, sign the back of the ticket. In most states a lottery ticket is a “bearer instrument” — whoever holds a signed ticket owns the claim, and an unsigned ticket can be claimed by whoever finds it. Photograph both sides, email the photos to yourself, and put the original in a fireproof safe or a bank safe-deposit box. Do not carry it around to show people.

2. Stay quiet

The single biggest mistake new winners make is announcing the win before claiming it. Every state has different anonymity rules: a few let jackpot winners stay fully anonymous, some allow claiming through a trust, and many require your name to be published. Until you know your state’s rules — and have a plan — tell only your spouse and, shortly, your lawyer. A quiet week now prevents years of requests, scams and strained friendships later.

3. Build your professional team

Before claiming a life-changing prize, hire three people: an attorney experienced in lottery or estate matters, a certified public accountant, and a fee-only financial planner. The attorney handles anonymity options, trusts and the claim itself. The accountant handles the tax shock — as our lottery tax guide explains, 24% is withheld up front but your real federal bill can reach 37%, plus state tax. Interview more than one of each, and never hire anyone who approaches you first.

4. Know your deadline — and use the time

Claim deadlines range from about 90 days to one year after the draw, depending on the state and the prize. That window is a gift: use it to do steps 1–3 properly. But mark the date in three calendars, because an expired jackpot ticket is worth nothing, and large prizes generally cannot be claimed at a shop — you will claim at a lottery district office or headquarters, sometimes by appointment.

5. Decide: lump sum or annuity

The advertised jackpot is the annuity value — 30 graduated payments over 29 years. The cash option is a single smaller payment (recently, often around 45–50% of the headline figure). The annuity wins on total dollars and built-in discipline; the lump sum wins on control, investment flexibility and estate planning, but concentrates all the tax in one year. There is no universally right answer — it depends on your age, debts, discipline and family. Model both, after tax, with your team.

6. Plan the money before it arrives

Decide in advance what the first year looks like: taxes reserved in a separate account, debts cleared, an emergency fund, and a strict “fun” allowance. Financial planners who work with winners often suggest parking the prize in plain savings for several months while the attention fades and your plan matures. Sudden-wealth regret almost always comes from month-one spending, not from the win itself.

7. Claim, then keep your life boring for a while

Claim the prize with your attorney present, complete the W-2G paperwork, and confirm exactly when and how payment will arrive. Then give yourself time. Change your phone number if requests flood in, be kind but firm with a prepared “we’re not discussing it,” and remember that the goal is not to feel rich for a summer — it is to be secure for life. Still in the “just checking” stage? Start with our guide on how to check your Powerball ticket.

Protect the claim while you prepare

While your team is being assembled, guard the paperwork: do not post photos of the ticket’s barcode or serial numbers, do not let the ticket leave your sight at a retailer, and be wary of anyone who contacts you offering to “help” claim. If your state allows claiming through a trust, your attorney may advise forming it before you sign anything else — the order of these steps can decide whether your name stays private.

Frequently asked questions

How long do I have to claim a jackpot?

Typically 180 days to one year from the draw date, set by the state where you bought the ticket. Some states give annuity and cash choices different deadlines for electing the cash option (often 60 days after claiming), so confirm early.

Can I stay anonymous?

It depends on the state. Some allow full anonymity for big winners, some allow a trust or LLC to claim, and some publish every winner’s name. An attorney in your state can tell you exactly what is possible before you claim.

What if I bought the ticket in a pool?

Get the pool’s agreement in writing before the draw whenever possible. At claim time, IRS Form 5754 lets the lottery pay each member separately with matching tax paperwork.
