Why Do Gas Prices Change Every Day?

Money Guide · NumberBuzz USA

You filled up on Monday at one price and drove past the same station on Thursday to find a different one. Gas prices feel random, but they follow a logic: roughly half of every gallon is crude oil, and the rest is refining, transport, taxes and the station’s own margin — and every one of those ingredients moves. Once you see how the pieces fit, the numbers on our 50-state gas table start to make sense: in our latest snapshot the national average was $3.81 a gallon, yet North Carolina drivers paid $3.09 while Hawaii paid $5.62.

1. Crude oil is the biggest ingredient

Roughly half the price of a gallon of gasoline is the price of crude oil, which trades on world markets every second. Wars, storms, OPEC decisions and global demand all move crude — and pump prices follow within days. When crude jumps 10%, expect the pump price to follow by roughly 20–25 cents a gallon, spread over a week or two. The trip is usually faster on the way up than on the way down, a pattern drivers call “rockets and feathers.”

2. Refineries and seasons

Crude has to be refined into gasoline, and refinery maintenance, unexpected outages or a hurricane on the Gulf Coast can squeeze supply overnight. Twice a year the blend itself changes: cleaner summer-blend fuel costs more to make, which is one reason prices often climb in spring, while the switch back to winter blend in the fall often brings a small drop. A single big refinery going offline can lift prices across a whole region within days.

3. Taxes and distance explain the state gaps

Every state adds its own fuel tax on top of the federal 18.4 cents a gallon, and the differences are large — some states add under 10 cents, others more than 50 cents once fees are included. Distance matters too: states far from refineries and pipelines — Hawaii, California, the Pacific Northwest — pay more for transport and for stricter local fuel rules. That is why the cheapest states in our table cluster in the South and Midwest, close to Gulf Coast refineries, while the priciest sit on the coasts. Our guide to the cheapest gas states in the USA ranks them all and explains the pattern state by state.

4. Demand moves the daily number

Holiday weekends, summer road trips and even a Friday payday push demand up; a quiet winter week pulls it down. Stations also price against the station across the street, which is why two blocks can mean a 30-cent difference on the same street. Wholesale prices change daily, and a station that refills its tanks on an expensive day will price higher than a rival who filled up cheap — until the next delivery levels things out.

How to find the cheapest fuel near you

Check the national and state averages on this page first, so you know what “fair” looks like today. Fill up early in the week — prices often rise toward the weekend. Avoid motorway service areas and airport stations, where convenience is priced in; warehouse clubs and busy supermarket stations are usually the cheapest. Keep your tyres properly inflated and clear heavy junk out of the trunk — both quietly improve your mileage. And when prices are falling, there is no rush to top up a half-full tank: waiting a day can genuinely save you money. For more ideas, see our 10 easy ways to save money on gas.

Why two stations on the same street charge differently

Even with identical wholesale costs, retail prices differ. A station on a busy corner pays more rent and can charge for convenience; a high-volume station near a supermarket can survive on a few cents of margin per gallon because it sells so much of it. Brand matters too — major-brand fuel with additive packages is priced above unbranded fuel, whether or not your car can tell the difference. This is why the average in our table is a guide, not a promise.

Frequently asked questions

Why did gas go up overnight at my station?

Most likely the station took a delivery priced at a higher wholesale rate, or crude oil jumped and the whole local market moved. Stations reprice when their replacement cost changes, not when the fuel in the ground changes.

Why is gas so much cheaper in the South?

Proximity to Gulf Coast refineries, lower state fuel taxes and cheaper land and transport. Southern and Midwestern states sit close to the supply; coastal states import fuel over long distances and tax it more heavily.

Do gas prices really change every day?

Wholesale prices do — they follow crude oil markets that trade constantly. Retail prices at a given station may change daily or sit still for a week; the AAA state averages on NumberBuzz are refreshed every morning so you can see the trend.

State averages shown on NumberBuzz come from AAA’s national survey of roughly 120,000 stations; they are indicative snapshots, and your local price will differ — sometimes by a lot.
