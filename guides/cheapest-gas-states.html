Cheapest Gas in the USA: Which States Pay the Least (And Why)

Money Guide · NumberBuzz USA

In the latest AAA snapshot on NumberBuzz (November 16, 2026), the cheapest gas in America was in North Carolina at $3.09 a gallon for regular, while the most expensive was Hawaii at $5.62 — a gap of more than $2.50 a gallon for the same fuel. Texas ($3.28), Indiana ($3.32), Georgia ($3.33) and Oklahoma ($3.33) rounded out the five cheapest states, against a national average of $3.81. Why does one state pay half again as much as another? Taxes, refineries and geography — here is the ranking and the reasons behind it. Prices change daily; check the live 50-state table on the homepage for today’s figures.

At the other end of the table sit the usual suspects: Hawaii ($5.62), California ($5.57), Nevada ($4.90), Washington ($4.83) and Oregon ($4.46). The pattern is not random — the cheap seats cluster in the South and Midwest, and the expensive seats line the Pacific coast and the islands.

Reason 1: State taxes and fees

Every gallon carries the federal tax of 18.4 cents, but state taxes range from under 10 cents a gallon in the lightest states to more than 60 cents once California’s taxes and environmental fees are stacked up. Pennsylvania, Illinois and New Jersey also sit near the top of the tax table, which is why their pump prices stay stubbornly above their neighbors’. Low-tax states start every price comparison with a built-in head start.

Reason 2: Distance from refineries

Most US refining capacity hugs the Gulf Coast. Texas and Louisiana refine far more than they burn, and pipelines radiate outward from there — so the Southeast and Midwest buy fuel that travelled a short, cheap distance. Hawaii imports every drop by ship. California is a “fuel island”: its special cleaner-burning blend is made by a limited set of refineries, so a single refinery outage can spike prices across the whole West Coast. Our explainer on why gas prices change every day digs into the refinery and seasonal side of the story.

Reason 3: Fuel rules and blends

California’s unique gasoline recipe, designed for air quality, costs more to produce and cannot be replaced by fuel from other states in a crunch. Parts of the Northeast and Midwest also require special blends in summer. Where rules are simpler and supply is flexible, prices are lower and calmer.

What it means for your wallet

For a driver filling a 14-gallon tank weekly, the difference between North Carolina’s $3.09 and California’s $5.57 is about $35 a fill — over $1,800 a year. Most of us cannot move states for cheaper gas, but the same forces work locally: prices fall as you move away from airports, motorways and wealthy suburbs, and warehouse clubs near refinery country are often the cheapest pumps in any state. Wherever you live, the tactics in our guide to 10 easy ways to save money on gas work the same.

The middle of the table tells its own story

Between the $3.09 floor and the $5.62 ceiling sits the crowded middle — states like Florida ($3.60), Colorado ($3.61), Iowa and Wisconsin ($3.63) and Michigan ($3.79) — where moderate taxes and decent refinery access keep prices near the national average. These are the states where weekly swings matter most to household budgets: close enough to the Gulf supply chain to benefit when crude falls, far enough away to feel it when a refinery sneezes.

Within every state, the same gaps repeat

State averages hide local drama. In almost every state, the priciest pumps cluster in the biggest city’s core, near airports and along interstate exits, while the cheapest sit in suburbs with warehouse clubs and competing stations on the same block. Rural drivers far from any competition can pay big-city prices with none of the convenience. The state ranking sets your expectations; your neighborhood decides your actual bill.

Frequently asked questions

Which state has the cheapest gas right now?

In the November 16, 2026 AAA snapshot, North Carolina at $3.09 a gallon. The title changes hands — Texas, Oklahoma and Georgia are frequent leaders — so check the homepage table for the current ranking.

Why is California gas so expensive?

A stack of the nation’s highest fuel taxes and fees, a special low-emission blend few refineries make, and isolation from other states’ supply. Any refinery problem in California stays in California.

Why is Hawaii the priciest?

Every gallon arrives by ship, volumes are small, and land and operating costs are high. Island geography is the most expensive geography there is.

State averages are AAA figures captured November 16, 2026, shown as indicative snapshots. Your local price will differ — sometimes by a lot — and rankings change daily.
